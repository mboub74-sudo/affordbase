Choosing between an FHSA and a TFSA for a Canadian down payment starts with two questions: do you qualify for an FHSA, and how certain are you that the money will be used for a qualifying home? An FHSA can offer a deduction for eligible contributions and tax-free qualifying withdrawals. A TFSA generally offers more flexibility for other goals, without a contribution deduction.

The accounts can work together. The better allocation depends on eligibility, available room, purchase timing, cash needs, and the investments held inside each account. This guide separates the tax wrapper from the savings product, provides original household examples, and highlights practical withdrawal mistakes. CRA guidance was reviewed on November 17, 2026; verify your own records before contributing or withdrawing.

## FHSA versus TFSA at a glance

An account name describes tax treatment, not a guaranteed return. Either account may hold different eligible products, depending on the provider. A cash deposit, a guaranteed investment certificate, and a stock portfolio have different risks and access restrictions even when placed inside the same type of registered account.

| Planning question | FHSA | TFSA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contribution deduction | Eligible contributions are generally deductible | Contributions are not deductible |
| Home-purchase withdrawal | Tax-free when all qualifying conditions are met | Generally tax-free; no first-home use requirement |
| Contribution capacity | Separate FHSA participation rules | Personal TFSA room based on eligibility and history |
| Using money for other goals | Ordinary withdrawals may be taxable | Generally more flexible tax-free withdrawals |
| Replacing a withdrawal | Do not assume TFSA-style restoration | Withdrawn amount returns as room next calendar year |

Use the table as a starting point rather than a complete tax checklist. Your available room is personal, and special transactions can change the calculation. Opening accounts at two banks does not give you two independent annual allowances. Keep a combined record of contributions, transfers, and withdrawals across all providers.

## Confirm FHSA eligibility before comparing benefits

CRA's opening conditions include Canadian residency, applicable age requirements, and a first-time-buyer test. The test considers whether you lived in a home owned by you or, in relevant circumstances, your spouse or common-law partner during the current year or previous four calendar years. Ownership and occupancy details matter; the phrase first home can be misleading by itself.

Someone who has never personally held title may still fail the opening test because of living in a partner-owned home. Conversely, the rules are not simply a lifetime ban after any past ownership. Review CRA's exact conditions for your situation rather than applying a slogan or assuming a mortgage preapproval establishes account eligibility.

Eligibility to open and eligibility to make a qualifying withdrawal are separate checks. Revisit the rules when buying, especially if your residency, relationship, or housing history has changed. A bank's willingness to open an account is not a substitute for giving accurate information and understanding the applicable conditions.

## Understand FHSA room without assuming unlimited catch-up

CRA states that first-year FHSA participation room is $8,000 in the year the first account is opened. General rules include a $40,000 lifetime limit and up to $8,000 of unused participation-room carryforward. Room does not accumulate for years before you open the first FHSA. Contributions and RRSP transfers share the applicable room.

For a simple example without special transactions, imagine opening an FHSA this year and contributing $3,000. The unused $5,000 can carry into the next year, potentially making $13,000 available then, subject to the lifetime limit and your actual record. Waiting several years without opening does not create the same history.

Do not confuse unused contribution room with an unused tax deduction. They answer different questions: how much can enter the account, and how much eligible contribution can be deducted for a particular tax year. CRA provides separate calculations. Maintain records and obtain advice for excess contributions, RRSP transfers, or other transactions that complicate the ordinary example.

## What the deduction can mean in dollars

An eligible deduction reduces taxable income rather than returning the whole contribution. For an illustration, assume an $8,000 deductible contribution and a constant effective marginal tax saving of 30% on that amount. The arithmetic gives $2,400 of tax savings. Actual savings depend on tax brackets, province, income, credits, and other deductions.

The contribution still requires $8,000 of available cash. Do not describe it as costing $5,600 today unless you have separately accounted for when the $2,400 benefit actually reaches your finances. A refund after filing and reduced withholding during the year have different cash-flow timing, and your actual tax return may include other amounts owing.

If that illustrative $2,400 is saved, it can help the wider home-buying fund. If it is spent on travel, the account still contains the original contribution, but the home goal does not receive the extra amount. Plan the use of any realized tax benefit explicitly rather than automatically adding an assumed refund to the down payment spreadsheet.

## When TFSA flexibility is particularly useful

A TFSA can be useful when the same savings might be needed for moving, education, an emergency, or a home. Contributions are not deductible, but ordinary withdrawals are generally tax-free. That flexibility can matter when you are unsure whether you will remain in the same city or buy within the FHSA's permitted timeframe.

The amount withdrawn from a TFSA is added back to contribution room on January 1 of the following calendar year. It does not instantly become new room on withdrawal day. If you recontribute during the same year, you need other available room. This distinction matters when moving money between institutions or temporarily taking out a deposit.

For example, someone with no unused room withdraws $6,000 in June. The withdrawal does not by itself authorize a $6,000 replacement contribution in September. The room returns the next calendar year. Use a properly arranged direct transfer when appropriate rather than treating withdrawal and redeposit as the same administrative action.

## An original savings allocation example

Consider a fictional eligible buyer who can save $900 monthly after essentials, required debt payments, irregular bills, and a separate emergency reserve. Assume the buyer opens an FHSA and has $8,000 available participation room. Annual savings capacity is $10,800. Ignoring returns, one allocation could place $8,000 in the FHSA and $2,800 in a TFSA with sufficient available room.

| Allocation | Annual amount | Purpose in this example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FHSA contributions | $8,000 | Money intended for a qualifying first-home purchase |
| TFSA savings | $2,800 | Flexible additional purchase or moving cash |
| Total new savings | $10,800 | $900 multiplied by twelve |

Monthly transfers could be scheduled at roughly $666.67 toward the FHSA and the remainder toward the TFSA, with a final adjustment so FHSA contributions do not exceed $8,000. Alternatively, the buyer could fund one account first. The schedule is an administrative choice; the annual room and available cash remain the constraints.

At a hypothetical 30% tax saving, the FHSA contribution might generate $2,400 of tax savings. Keep that amount outside the base $10,800 until it is actually available. If saved later, check the destination account's remaining room first. Do not count it as both part of the year's $900 monthly surplus and an additional contribution.

## Keep your purchase target broader than the down payment

Suppose a buyer sets aside $35,000 for the down payment, $8,000 for estimated closing and setup expenses, and $10,000 as a post-purchase reserve. The combined target is $53,000. These are invented planning amounts, not a closing-cost estimate for a particular province or property. Obtain local estimates for taxes, legal costs, adjustments, and moving.

If current designated savings are $20,000, the remaining gap is $33,000. Saving $900 monthly closes that gap in about 36.7 months before returns, tax benefits, or changes in costs. Rounding up gives approximately thirty-seven monthly contributions. A favorable tax outcome may shorten the path, but the base plan should remain visible.

Use the [down-payment savings guide](/articles/save-for-house-down-payment/) to build the complete target and the [closing-cost guide](/articles/closing-costs-500000-home-canada/) to identify categories requiring local verification. Do not let the account comparison distract from whether the monthly ownership budget works after the purchase.

## Match investments to when the money is needed

The account's tax benefits do not protect investment value. A buyer who needs a deposit in six months faces a different problem from someone with a flexible ten-year horizon. A stock-market decline immediately before purchase can reduce available cash even when every contribution and withdrawal follows the tax rules.

Compare principal stability, liquidity, maturity dates, fees, and the time required to access funds. A non-redeemable GIC might mature after the expected deposit date. A cash product may have a lower expected return but fewer short-term price fluctuations. Ask the provider exactly what can be withdrawn, when, and at what cost.

Avoid comparing a risky TFSA portfolio with an FHSA cash deposit and concluding that one account type earns more. That comparison changes the investment as well as the wrapper. To isolate tax treatment, compare similar holdings and fees; then separately decide what risk is appropriate for the goal's timing.

## Plan qualifying withdrawals before an offer deadline

For an FHSA, check CRA's qualifying-withdrawal conditions and the provider's required form before requesting funds. Conditions include a qualifying Canadian home, a written purchase or construction agreement with specified timing, residency requirements, and an intention to occupy the home as a principal residence within the required period. The exact dates matter.

Do not assume a regular cash withdrawal becomes tax-free merely because you later use the money for a house. Ask the provider how to process the qualifying withdrawal and how long sale settlement or cash release takes. Keep the purchase agreement and withdrawal documentation with the transaction records.

The deposit required with an offer can come earlier than final closing. Make a calendar showing the offer deposit, account liquidation, withdrawal processing, and closing transfer. Arrange accessible cash for each stage. A large account balance is not enough if the money cannot reach the lawyer or deposit recipient by the deadline.

## What if you do not buy a home?

An FHSA has a maximum participation period rather than remaining open indefinitely. CRA describes closure deadlines linked to the fifteenth anniversary of opening, age seventy-one, or the year after a first qualifying withdrawal. Review the exact deadline that applies to you and do not wait until the last week to ask about available options.

Subject to the rules, a direct transfer to your RRSP or RRIF can generally defer tax without requiring ordinary RRSP contribution room; later withdrawals are subject to that plan's rules. An ordinary taxable FHSA withdrawal is different. Ask the provider to explain the appropriate process rather than withdrawing first and trying to repair the tax treatment afterward.

If a home purchase is uncertain, weigh that retirement-transfer possibility against the TFSA's broader access. Neither feature makes one account universally best. A saver who may need tuition cash soon has different priorities from someone comfortable moving unused home savings toward retirement. Compare realistic alternate uses, not only the preferred outcome.

## Use both accounts without duplicating the same dollars

Maintain a single home-buying worksheet with columns for account balance, accessible amount, intended use, and earliest availability. Include any restrictions on the holdings. Separate emergency savings from money committed to the purchase, even if both happen to be held within TFSAs. One dollar cannot fund both the deposit and a repair reserve.

If buying with another person, track eligibility, contribution room, and account ownership individually. Two eligible buyers may each have an FHSA, but one person's unused room does not become the other's. Decide how contributions and ownership arrangements will be documented rather than assuming a shared goal removes the separate tax rules.

The Home Buyers' Plan is another distinct tool, not another name for a TFSA or FHSA. CRA permits combining an eligible HBP withdrawal with an FHSA qualifying withdrawal for the same home when all conditions are met. Because HBP repayment obligations differ, review them separately before relying on RRSP money in the purchase plan.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should I always max out an FHSA before a TFSA?

No automatic order fits everyone. FHSA eligibility, purchase plans, emergency access, tax circumstances, and available room all matter. An eligible buyer with a clear home goal may value the deduction, while someone needing flexible near-term cash may prioritize TFSA savings. Protect essential spending and verify contribution capacity before either transfer.

### Can I use an FHSA and a TFSA for the same house?

Yes, provided the FHSA withdrawal meets its qualifying conditions and transactions follow each account's rules. Track both balances and processing times. The TFSA does not make an otherwise taxable FHSA withdrawal qualifying, and withdrawing from one account does not create contribution room in the other.

### Does opening an FHSA create a guaranteed tax refund?

No. Opening is different from making an eligible deductible contribution, and a deduction is different from a guaranteed refund. The tax effect depends on your return and the year you claim the deduction. Use a tax estimate for your circumstances and do not spend an assumed refund before confirming it.

### Is money inside either account guaranteed safe?

The tax wrapper does not guarantee investment value or immediate access. Review the underlying product, provider, applicable deposit protection, market risk, and withdrawal terms. A cash deposit and a stock fund have different characteristics even inside the same account. Choose holdings with the actual purchase date and cash requirements in mind.
