Canada's Groceries and Essentials Benefit, or CGEB, provides income-tested quarterly support. Budget 2026 also proposes a separate supplement for early 2027. These are different payments: the regular benefit already operates, while the budget describes the new supplement as a proposal. A headline combining several measures is not the amount every person will receive in one deposit.

This guide separates the payment streams, explains which dates are published, and shows how to turn a confirmed entitlement into a grocery cash-flow plan. Information was reviewed on November 16, 2026. Use your CRA notice for your personal amount and check official updates before relying on the proposed supplement. All household budgets below are invented Canadian-dollar examples.

## What is the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit?

The [CRA's CGEB overview](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-groceries-essentials-benefit.html) explains that the program replaced the GST/HST credit in July 2026. It supplies tax-free quarterly payments for eligible individuals and families with low or modest incomes. The benefit increased by 25% in July 2026 for a five-year period. Its regular eligibility and calculation structure continues from the former credit.

That history matters when comparing older articles. An article about the former GST/HST credit, a June 2026 top-up, and a November budget announcement can all describe related support without referring to the same payment. Match the announcement date, benefit year and payment type before combining numbers. Keep related provincial payments separately identified as well.

For your household, the useful starting point is the entitlement CRA has assessed. Record the annual amount, the period it covers and the expected instalments. A search result advertising a maximum cannot show the outcome of your income and family circumstances. Use the maximum to understand the program's scale, then use the notice to plan spending.

## What Budget 2026 proposes for early 2027

[Budget 2026's affordability chapter](https://budget.canada.ca/2026/report-rapport/chap3-en.html) describes an additional one-time 50% enhancement for the 2026–27 benefit year, intended for early 2027. Its summary gives approximate maximum supplements of $339 for a single person without children and $679 for a couple with two children. Those figures describe the proposed extra payment, not the regular annual benefit.

The same chapter combines the proposed supplement, the June 2026 top-up and the enhanced regular benefit to describe total support of about $1,290 or $2,570 for those example households. These are combined headline totals across specified measures. They should not be labelled a universal 2027 cheque or a new monthly grocery allowance.

The budget's [technical tax-measures annex](https://budget.canada.ca/2026/report-rapport/tm-mf-en.html) specifies separate proposed components and phase-in and phase-out adjustments. Rounded component figures do not provide a dependable personal calculator. Do not simply multiply your current quarterly deposit by two, or assume the proposed amount equals half your own annual entitlement. Await the applicable final rules and CRA's calculation.

| Regular CGEB | July 2026 onward | An operating quarterly benefit with assessed eligibility |
| Proposed 2027 supplement | Intended early 2027 | Budget proposal; do not invent a personal award or exact deposit date |

The [CRA's June-payment announcement](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/2026/04/canada-groceries-and-essentials-benefit-one-time-top-up-payment-to-make-groceries-and-other-essentials-more-affordable-is-coming-june-5.html) identifies June 5, 2026 for the earlier top-up. That date cannot establish when a different 2027 supplement will arrive. A sensible payment tracker has one row for each stream, with the official source and the date you last checked it.

Add a status column: received, confirmed but pending, or proposed. Only money received is available in your bank account. A confirmed future payment can support a dated plan, while a proposed payment should remain outside commitments that must be paid regardless. This distinction is particularly useful when rent and groceries compete for the same cash.

## Who qualifies for the regular benefit?

The [CRA eligibility page](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-groceries-essentials-benefit/who-eligible.html) requires Canadian tax residency in the month before a payment and at the start of the payment month. Generally, applicants must be at least nineteen, with exceptions for younger people who have or had a spouse or common-law partner, or who are or were parents living with their child.

Income also matters. For the 2025 base year, CRA lists maximum adjusted-family-net-income thresholds of $60,012 for a single person without children and $73,592 for a couple with two children. Being below a threshold does not establish a maximum award. Other eligibility conditions and the calculation still apply. Immigration status and tax residency are not interchangeable concepts.

If you are uncertain about residence, a change in family situation or which children are included, use the linked CRA guidance or obtain qualified help. Do not select a convenient household category just to reproduce a headline amount. Accurate information reduces the risk of an incorrect assessment and a later adjustment to payments you already budgeted.

## How much is the regular CGEB?

The CRA's [payment-amount table](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-groceries-essentials-benefit/how-much/payment-amounts.html) lists 2026–27 maximum components of $445 per eligible adult and $234 per eligible child, with an additional single-person component subject to its conditions. These produce headline annual maxima of $679 for a qualifying single adult and $1,358 for a qualifying couple with two eligible children.

Those are regular-benefit figures for July 2026 through June 2027, not additional money on top of the same regular payments. Family income can reduce the award, and the single-person component has its own phase-in. The tax-measures annex describes a 5% regular phase-out above $46,432 of 2025 adjusted family net income. Your assessed notice remains the practical authority for personal planning.

Keep annual and quarterly figures labelled. If an invented notice shows a regular annual entitlement of $600 paid evenly, that is $150 per quarter. It is not $600 each quarter. If a separate payment arrives, record it as another transaction rather than changing the recurring quarterly amount unless CRA actually changes that entitlement.

## Which 2027 payment dates are confirmed?

The [government benefits calendar](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/calendar.html) lists regular CGEB dates of January 5, April 5, July 5 and October 5, 2027. These are regular quarterly dates. The calendar does not, by listing them, assign the proposed supplement to January 5. Keep the phrase “early 2027” separate from a specific day until an official supplement schedule establishes one.

Also distinguish the calendar year from the benefit year. January and April sit within the July 2026–June 2027 period. The July instalment begins a new benefit period whose assessed amount may differ. Do not assume all four 2027 deposits will equal the first one, especially if the tax information or household circumstances used in the calculation changes.

On your calendar, add both the official payment date and the bills you expect to fund. If the payment has not arrived, check the official payment-status instructions before escalating. Avoid assigning a not-yet-received deposit to purchases that cannot wait. A small cash buffer, when feasible, makes a timing difference less disruptive.

## Filing and household information: what to check

The CRA overview says filing your tax return triggers consideration for the benefit and related programs. Its [how-to-get-the-benefit guidance](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/child-family-benefits/canada-groceries-essentials-benefit/get-benefit.html) should be your starting point, particularly for new residents. Do not assume every newcomer follows the same process as someone already assessed through an existing Canadian return.

Make a short administration list: required return filed, assessment available, address current, family information correct, and payment method checked. Use CRA's own instructions for updates. Keep confirmation documents somewhere you can retrieve them. A completed checklist does not guarantee an award, but it helps identify missing information when a payment differs from what you expected.

Protect account details when looking for assistance. Read program information on official government pages and navigate to the official sign-in rather than following an unsolicited message promising a grocery payment. No calculator on AffordBase needs your social insurance number, banking login or CRA password. Use public examples here and personal records within the appropriate official service.

## Turn a quarterly payment into a grocery spending plan

Suppose an invented household receives $150 for a three-month period. Dividing by three gives a $50 monthly planning allowance, while dividing by thirteen gives about $11.54 weekly. Neither conversion changes the actual quarterly deposit schedule. Choose one method for your worksheet so you do not allocate the same payment to monthly and weekly spending twice.

Consider keeping the deposit in a separate budget category and releasing the planned amount gradually. If the first week's shop uses $60 of it, record that withdrawal and the remaining $90. A running balance is more reliable than remembering that you “still have the benefit money” after several ordinary purchases have already drawn it down.

For a hypothetical separate $300 supplement, one possible allocation is $120 for an immediate essentials gap, $90 reserved for later grocery weeks and $90 for an overdue essential bill. This is an example of assigning dollars, not advice on a particular entitlement. Adjust priorities to urgent needs and avoid treating one-time support as permanent income.

## Combine support with realistic grocery savings

Before the next shop, check what you already own and choose meals that fit the coming week. A useful shopping list contains missing ingredients, not a duplicate pantry. Compare unit prices for frequent purchases and buy quantities you expect to use. These practical steps complement support; they cannot resolve every household's food shortfall.

## Keep a dated benefit record

Save the notice or official update with the date you reviewed it and the period it covers. If a new announcement appears, compare what actually changed: eligibility, amount, timing or proposal status. Update only the affected budget line. This avoids replacing a confirmed quarterly plan with a headline that describes a different payment stream.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the 2027 Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit a $1,290 cheque?

No. The budget's approximate figure combines specified support measures for an example single household. It is not a universal one-time award. Separate your regular entitlement from the proposed supplement, and use CRA's actual notice and payment information before setting a spending limit based on a personal amount.

### Does the new supplement arrive on January 5, 2027?

January 5 is a published regular quarterly CGEB date. The reviewed budget proposal describes the extra supplement as intended for early 2027 without establishing that same exact deposit day. Check official updates for the supplement rather than turning a regular calendar entry into an unsupported payment promise.

### Can I receive support if I have no employment income?

The eligibility guidance says people may qualify even without income, subject to residency, age, family and income-tested rules. Employment is not the only consideration. Follow the filing or newcomer instructions that apply to you and check the resulting assessment rather than assuming a zero salary guarantees the maximum.

### How can I save money on groceries while waiting?

Build the next shopping list around existing food, compare usable quantities and unit prices, and protect a realistic amount for essentials. Keep a proposed payment outside the cash available today. Where the current budget cannot cover adequate food, seek suitable benefits or local support instead of relying on unconfirmed money.
